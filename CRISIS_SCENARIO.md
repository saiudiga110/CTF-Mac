# vBank Cyber Crisis Scenario

The CFO receives an alert that a corporate client's high-value payment never reached its beneficiary. Within the hour, three more clients report the same thing — settlements that show as sent on vBank's side but have simply never arrived on the other end. Customer balances appear correct. The money hasn't vanished from the ledger; it just isn't landing where it's supposed to. Speculation starts building on social media before the bank has said a word, and corporate treasury teams begin calling their relationship managers for an explanation nobody can yet give them.

The bank's executive team assembles an emergency response group. The early read is measured relief: nothing has been stolen from vBank's own balance sheet. The read that follows is worse. Attackers didn't take money directly — they moved through a chain of ordinary web application and API weaknesses across the customer platform and the internal systems behind it, manipulating how payments are screened and routed until compliance holds meant nothing and staff-only tools answered to whoever asked. No single flaw did this. A sequence of small, unremarkable ones did, each trusted a little more than it had earned.

As the crisis unfolds, participants must work together to:

- Identify the point of compromise and the full chain of web application and API vulnerabilities an attacker used to move from an anonymous visitor to a trusted internal session.
- Commission focused web application security testing and API security testing across every customer- and staff-facing service to confirm which findings are exploited, which are latent, and what else shares the same root cause.
- Trace attacker activity across customer-facing, staff, and backend environments to establish how far access actually extended.
- Protect sensitive customer and payment data for the duration of the response, without destroying the evidence needed to understand what happened.
- Restore critical banking services and payment integrity while minimising disruption to customers who did nothing wrong.
- Manage regulatory, operational, and reputational risk in parallel — not in sequence — as facts are still emerging.
- Make time-critical decisions under pressure, with incomplete information, knowing that every option on the table costs something.

Every decision made in the next few hours will shape customer trust, revenue, regulatory standing, and market confidence for far longer than the incident itself lasts. Shutting a system down protects data but halts legitimate payments. Disclosing early protects trust but invites scrutiny before the picture is complete. Waiting for certainty protects the narrative but costs the bank the initiative.

This is the reality facing modern financial institutions: cybersecurity is not only a technology problem, it is a business resilience challenge. The vulnerabilities behind an incident like this are rarely exotic — they are the kind that disciplined web application security testing and API security testing are specifically designed to catch before an attacker finds them first. Closing the gap, and staying closed, takes more than a patch. It takes executives, business leaders, risk teams, and security professionals working from the same picture, at the same time, to protect customers and keep essential banking services running.

**Objective:** Lead the bank through a cyber crisis, safeguard customer trust, maintain operational resilience, and ensure the secure continuation of critical financial services.

**Key Themes:** Customer Trust • Operational Resilience • Fraud Prevention • Web Application Security Testing • API Security Testing • Crisis Management • Regulatory Compliance • Executive Decision-Making
